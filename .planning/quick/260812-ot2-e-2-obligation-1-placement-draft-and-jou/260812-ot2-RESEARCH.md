## Pre-placement check — "correction-and-disclosure" wording in a FRESH submission (cross-venue)

**Finding (uniform across all 11 venues): no policy machinery exists that treats the word "correction" inside a fresh, never-submitted manuscript as a formal post-publication correction notice.** Corrections/errata/amendment workflows (Nature Portfolio "Corrections & Amendments," Cell Press/Elsevier corrections, BMC corrections, PLOS corrections, eLife record amendments, per COPE guidance) attach exclusively to **published or in-press** articles (model knowledge — verify per venue policy page; HIGH confidence on the structural point, this is definitional to those policies). The v2 file's pre-paste check concerns an *in-submission* manuscript; a fresh submission has no live editorial record for correction machinery to act on. In a fresh submission, a Methods subsection disclosing an analysis-history exposure reads editorially as a transparency/rigor disclosure — the same genre as Cell Press's designated "Limitations of the study" subsection and Nature Portfolio's mandatory Reporting Summary (both designated transparency surfaces; model knowledge — verify).

**Per-venue safer-read column (where A's Limitations-only placement is the safer read):**
- **Nature flagship only:** Methods is capped at ~3,000 words (verified) — a tightly budgeted Methods makes the Limitations-adjacent read the more natural home there. This is moot in practice given desk-reject likelihood.
- **All other 10 venues:** Methods placement is native; Cell Press venues (AJHG, Cell Genomics, HGG Advances) additionally have the designated Limitations subsection, so the §1 pointer-sentence-in-Limitations pattern maps directly onto house style.
- Readability note (not policy): a triage editor seeing "Correction" in a section title may briefly scan for a referenced published paper; this is a comprehension cost only, with no procedural consequence anywhere.

This memo asserts nothing about whether such a submission exists.

## Cross-cutting answer — 3 venues best combining reputation with realistic fit

For a solo-author audit paper with a strong equity/ancestry angle (AFR underrepresentation, All of Us follow-on):

1. **AJHG** — ASHG's flagship is the field's natural home for statistical-genetics audits and has made ancestry equity a core editorial theme; solo-author methods-rigor papers are within its normal register; hybrid model gives a $0 route. Best reputation-per-realistic-odds.
2. **Nature Communications** — the highest-reputation venue on the list with a genuine (not flattering) chance of acceptance for corrective work at this scale; and it is the natural landing of the Carter-directed Nature-first cascade (formal transfer with editor referral), so the ladder costs one desk-reject cycle, not a restart. APC $7,350 is the price of the brand.
3. **Genome Biology** — reproducibility/benchmarking audits are its core identity, the reviewer pool actually reads LD-methods arguments, and its genomics-methods reputation is arguably second only to the Nature titles for this specific paper genre. The equity angle lands as pipeline-generalizability (Track B AoU-AFR panel) rather than as a headline, which matches this journal's register.

**Honest ladder consistent with the Nature-first directive:** Nature (expect fast desk reject; take the transfer) → Nature Genetics (optional single rung; low odds) → **Nature Communications** → **AJHG** / **Genome Biology** → Genome Medicine (incumbent target; fully viable, ranked below GB on methods-nativeness) → PLOS Genetics / HGG Advances (high-probability floor). **eLife** is a parallel-philosophy option, not a rung: maximal rigor-transparency, but no accept gate and no Impact Factor since the model change — a real cost against the "most reputable" criterion for a solo-author career artifact. **Nature Metabolism: pruned** (weakest scope fit on the list).
